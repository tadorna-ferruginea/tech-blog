Honk, welcome to my pond!

Here I will collect some glimmering fragments: physics, mathematics, algorithms, films, recipes, illustrations, music, books, car repairs, and ways to improve one’s quality of life on a tight budget, among other things. I feel the need to keep a record because my life seems to have arrived at a stretch of water where rewards are sparse, where every direction looks like circling in place. Then I drift into distant branches of a river, caught in the nets of unpredictable fate and cast onto another unfamiliar surface. I can hardly do any particular thing to influence my own life; so writing something becomes the smallest dose of resistance I can offer to this absurdity.

> A guest said: “Like an ephemeron sent between heaven and earth, like a single grain of millet lost in the vast blue sea. I grieve at the brevity of my life and envy the endless Yangtze. I would travel with immortals and embrace the bright moon until the end. Knowing this cannot be attained all at once, I entrust my lingering echoes to the mournful wind.”
>
> Master Su said: “Only the clear breeze over the river and the bright moon among the mountains—heard by the ear, they become sound; met by the eye, they become colour. Take them without restraint; use them without exhaustion. They are the inexhaustible treasury of the Creator, shared for our delight.”

For quite a long time, I could not write anything at all. I was unwilling to face my own limits; I was afraid, and I kept exhausting myself in the struggle. To write this introduction, I returned to *Former Ode on the Red Cliffs* and found myself newly moved by it. That I can begin to write again has much to do with reaching a kind of reconciliation with my own ignorance. It has given me room to breathe, and to observe and describe one small fragment of the world I inhabit. I am still not as good at certain things as I once hoped to be, and perhaps I never will be. But I do have sincere friendships, I cherish my life, I am still easily moved, and I remain curious about what I do not know. I think I ought to be happy.

> The guest laughed with delight, washed the cups, and poured again. When the dishes and fruit were gone and the cups and plates lay scattered, we leaned upon one another in the boat, unaware that the east had already grown pale.

All quotations in this post are from Su Shi’s *Former Ode on the Red Cliffs*.
